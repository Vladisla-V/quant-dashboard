Return distribution

{{ distribution_chart | safe }}

Bars are the realized spread of daily returns for each series, normalized to a density so both samples are comparable, and the smooth curves are the normal distributions implied by each series' mean and standard deviation. Gaps between the bars and each series' own curve are what skewness and excess kurtosis measure - both relative to that series' shape, not the other series: negative skew means the left tail is longer, and positive excess kurtosis means fat tails relative to a normal. The dashed lines mark the 5% VaR, the return that only the worst 5% of days fell below, and CVaR 5% is the average return across those worst days. Both are shown as returns, so losses are negative.

Each point is one trading day and the line is the least-squares fit (portfolio − rf) = alpha + beta × (benchmark − rf). Both sides are net of the risk-free column in the uploaded file, so alpha is Jensen's alpha: the daily return earned beyond what the portfolio's market exposure alone would have paid. Annualized alpha scales that daily intercept by 252 trading days, and R² is the share of portfolio excess-return variance the benchmark explains.
